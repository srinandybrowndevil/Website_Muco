Interfaces people can use without being trained.

Flows, wireframes, interface design and a reusable design system, tested against real screen sizes and accessibility requirements rather than a designer's monitor. Based in Erode, Tamil Nadu, with remote delivery available.

What you get

Everything below is stated explicitly in your scope, with anything excluded named just as clearly. Nothing here is implied and then invoiced.

  • User flows and information architecture
  • Wireframes, then interface design for every state

How it runs

Discovery and goals

We start with what the business needs to happen, not with a feature list. What is the outcome, who is it for, and how will we know it worked?

Scope, proposal and milestones

A written scope with what is included, what is not, the milestone plan and the price. Nothing starts until you have that in hand.

Design and technical architecture

Interface design and the architecture underneath it — data model, roles, integrations — reviewed with you before code.

Development, QA and review

Built in milestones you can see. Every milestone is tested and reviewed with you before the next one starts.

Launch, measurement and support

Deployment, analytics, handover documentation and a support arrangement — so the project does not end the day it goes live.

Questions

About ui/ux and product design

Can you design without building it?

Yes. We hand over a specification another team can build from. We would rather do that well than hold a project hostage.

What if we already have a design?

We review it against how it will actually be built and used, tell you what will not survive contact with real screens, and fix those parts. We do not redesign work that is already good.

Do you test with real users?

Where the budget allows, yes, and it is worth arguing for. Five real users will find problems an internal review never will.

02 Schedule and standards

What decides the timeline, and what we hold ourselves to

What decides the schedule

Migration and reconciliation take longer than people expect, and running the new system alongside the old one for a period is usually worth the extra weeks. Staff training is part of the schedule rather than an afterthought: a system the people entering data do not trust gets worked around, and then the numbers are wrong in a new way.

Performance, security and handover

  • Tested on real devices, including a low-end Android phone, not only a desktop browser
  • Contrast and keyboard operation checked rather than assumed
  • HTTPS, security headers and dependency patching wherever we build or host
  • A written handover document, so the next person is not reverse-engineering it
  • Source code, design files and every account transferred on final payment

04 Questions

About crm, erp, hrms, lms & billing

Can you move our existing data across?

Usually yes, from spreadsheets, Tally exports or an older system. Migration is scoped and priced as its own task because it is real work and it deserves testing.

Does it handle GST properly?

Invoicing is built to your actual filing requirements, confirmed with whoever handles your accounts. We build to what your accountant needs, not to a generic template.

Can staff use it without training?

That is a design requirement, not an afterthought. If your team needs a manual to record a sale, the interface is wrong.

Can we keep using Tally or our accountant's system?

Usually yes. Most businesses do not want to change accountants to change software, so the practical approach is exports and reconciliation rather than replacement. Which system it has to talk to, and what your accountant needs out of it, is settled during discovery.

What if staff will not use it?

That is the most common way these projects fail, and it is a design problem rather than a training problem. We review the screens with the people who will actually enter the data before the build finishes, and we start with one workflow instead of switching everything at once.

Will it work on a slow or dropped connection?

It can, and whether it should is a real decision rather than a default. Offline capability costs engineering effort, so it is worth it for a shop floor or a delivery route and wasted for an office desk. We ask where it will actually be used.
